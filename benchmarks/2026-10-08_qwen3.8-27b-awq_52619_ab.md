## Why the kernel win doesn't land (TP-sharding correction)

The pre-bench prediction ("down_proj at decode M=2") used UNSHARDED K.
Under TP=2, `down_proj` is row-parallel sharded: per-GPU K = 17408/2 =
**8704**. So per-GPU K·M: M=2 → 17408 (regular HIP in BOTH legs —
c=1/c=2 are correct negative controls), M=4 → 34816 (the ONLY
newly-enabled e2e shape), M=5 → 43520 (Triton both). All other linears
(gate/up col-parallel K=5120, qkv/o, GDN projs) never enter the window
at M≤5.

Even at M=4 the saving is one linear (per-GPU down_proj, ~50–100 µs of
~74 ms steps) on a hybrid whose step time is dominated by 48 GDN
recurrent layers + attention — below bench noise. A `torch.compile`
freezing hypothesis was explicitly tested and ruled out: a probe calling
`_rdna_hybrid_w4a16_apply_impl` under `torch.compile(dynamic=True)`
routes M=1..4 → hip, M=5 → triton, identical to eager
(`/tmp/opencode/compile_dispatch_probe.py`).

## Decision: KEEP the patch

Rationale: verified correct + faster kernel, correct dispatch
(eager and compiled), zero e2e risk (inert except the single linear at a single M value; inert on live FP8 which never selects the kernel),
upstream-merged so it drops automatically at the v0.31.1 bump. Same
precedent as the carried-but-inert #50409 patch. Any future M=4
decode-heavy workload on the trial profile gets the faster kernel for
free. The pre-bench "4–6%" estimate is withdrawn — do not quote it.
