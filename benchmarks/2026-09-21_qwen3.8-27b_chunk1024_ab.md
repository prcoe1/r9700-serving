# 2026-09-21 — chunk-size A/B: 1024 vs 2048 for concurrent long-context decode

**TL;DR**: at c2 with a 112k co-prefill, chunk 1024 halves the co-decoder stall
(B-prefill p50 801→429ms, p99 1229→802ms) for +8% prefill TTFT (60.8→65.9s).
Kept 1024 — smoothness wins for concurrent long-context work.

## Setup

- Same stack as the morning c2 depth bench (v0.29.0, MTP3, no-async, V2, fp8 KV,
  128k max-len, TP2). Only `VLLM_MAX_BATCHED_TOKENS` changed (restart between arms).
- Method: `benchmarks/conc_itl_probe.py qwen3.8-27b 110000` — A streams 512 tokens
  from a 16k prompt; B (112.7k prompt) fires after 32 batches; A's inter-arrival
  split into pre-B / B-prefill / post-B windows.

## Results (A inter-arrival ms per batch; ~2.6–2.9 tokens/batch via MTP3)

| window | 2048 | 1024 |
|:-------|-----:|-----:|
| pre-B p50 / p99 | 44.0 / 46.9 | 43.8 / 49.2 |
| B-prefill p50 / p99 | 801.4 / 1229.4 | 428.8 / 801.6 |
| choke (p99 during / p50 before) | 27.9x | 18.3x |
| B TTFT (112.7k prompt) | 60.8 s | 65.9 s (+8%) |

Note: at 1024, A delivered its full 512-token stream *during* B's prefill
(post-B n=0 vs n=85 at 2048) — itself a smoothness signal. The 09-03 "below 2048
gains nothing" prediction does not hold for ITL smoothness; halving the chunk
halves the stall. Re-run on any vLLM bump or when #45238/#53479/#51599 land.
